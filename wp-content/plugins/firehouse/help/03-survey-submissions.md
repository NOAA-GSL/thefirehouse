# Reviewing a survey form submission

<!-- help audience: editors; screens: project-list, project-edit -->

What to do when a researcher sends the FireHouse survey form: find their submission, check it, then publish it or set it aside.

## When a submission arrives

While an administrator has the survey form turned on (FireHouse → Settings), each submission arrives as a draft project. Nothing a researcher sends appears on the site until one of you publishes it.

The team mailbox gets an email, "New FireHouse submission", with a link to the draft and a link to every draft. The email holds nothing the researcher wrote: open the draft to read it. If the researcher said the project has no IRB approval or exemption, the email's subject says so.

## Find it

- Choose the link in the email, or go to **FireHouse → Projects** and choose **Draft** at the top of the list.
- At the bottom of the **From the survey** box, a submission's **Response ID** starts with W_. A project brought in on the Import screen has another kind of ID.

## Check it

**From the survey** shows the researcher's answers exactly as they sent them. Check that the project meets the form's inclusion criteria:

- it's about end users and fire weather, in any phase of the fire cycle;
- it's finished;
- any research with people in it was approved by an IRB, or exempt;
- it's one project, not a researcher's whole body of work.

Soon after a submission arrives, while it's still a draft, each publication the researcher gave by DOI gets its title, journal and year under **Publications**. One that couldn't be looked up shows only its DOI or address, and so does every publication in a submission without IRB approval.

If the researcher answered any of the private questions, the **Private answers** box on the right holds the submitter's email, their job title, and how they've shared their results. Only the FireHouse team and the site's administrators see it, and it never appears on the site. Use the email to follow up with the researcher. These questions are optional, so a submission may have no email.

## Publish it

Fill in the editorial fields and publish it as you would any project: see [Reviewing and publishing a project](02-projects.md).

A submission without IRB approval or exemption can't be published. It stays a draft, marked "No IRB approval" in the Projects list.

## Correct it

You can't change a researcher's answers. If one needs correcting:

- **If it has never been published:** ask the researcher, if they gave an email, to send the form again with the correction. When the new draft arrives, delete the first one, as below.
- **If it has been published, even if it's a draft again now:** don't delete it. Its address, your editorial fields and the synthesis's links to it would go with it, and a new submission doesn't bring them back. Tell the developer instead.

## Set it aside or delete it

- **If it doesn't meet the criteria:** leave it as a draft while the team decides, or delete it.
- **If it's junk or a test, or was sent twice:** delete it, keeping the newer of two.

To delete a submission, choose **Move to Trash**, then open **Trash** in FireHouse → Projects and choose **Delete Permanently** under its title. Deleting it permanently also deletes its private answers, and so does WordPress emptying the Trash, which it does by itself, usually 30 days after something is put there. While the project is kept, its private answers are kept too, and you can't clear them. How long they may be kept waits on NOAA's privacy review.

The form turns away most automated submissions. If junk keeps getting through, tell an administrator.

To reword a question on the form, see [FireHouse settings](07-settings.md).
